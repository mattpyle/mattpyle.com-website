On a phone, a post opened with its title, its lead and the byline. Then came the whole side rail, stacked as one column: six to nine "On this page" links, one per row, and five action buttons. The first sentence of the post started 1,214 to 1,594px down. On the WebMCP post that is nearly two screens.

Most people reach a post from a shared link or a search result, on a phone. A newcomer who meets two screens of links before the writing is likely to leave before the post gets a chance.

The first change collapsed the contents list behind an "On this page" summary on phones, and laid the action buttons in a row. On a wide screen the rail looked as before. One post of three then started on the first phone screen. The other two still did not, so the work went back to open.

The change also did something nobody had checked for. The list was drawn open, and a script closed it after the page appeared. On a slow phone that moved the page under the reader's thumb. A second change serves the list already closed, so nothing moves on load. In tests on a slowed phone, the worst layout shift fell from 0.147 to 0.0083.
