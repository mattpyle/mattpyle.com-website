Pressing Shift+Tab moved focus up the WebMCP post on a phone. The browser scrolled each newly focused link only just into view, so it landed at the top of the screen, behind the 48 pixel sticky header.

The header is translucent and blurred, so what sits under it is unreadable. Of 41 stops on the post, one link was fully hidden and six were partly hidden. The homepage had 3 fully and 9 partly hidden stops.

This let down anyone moving by keyboard. A sticky header that hides a focused element fails WCAG 2.4.11.

Nothing reserved the header's height for focus scrolling. The page now sets an 80 pixel scroll padding at the top, which covers the 48 pixel phone header and the 72 pixel desktop one.
