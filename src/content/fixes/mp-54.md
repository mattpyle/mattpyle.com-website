Every post has an "On this page" contents block in its side rail. On sight it reads as a list: a column of separate links, one per section. In the markup it was not a list. On `/writing/accessibility-and-ai/`, 0 of its 6 contents links sat inside a list item. On the WebMCP post, 0 of 9 did.

This let down people who use a screen reader. On a list, a screen reader says how many items it holds, such as "list, 6 items". A list-navigation key also moves past the whole list in one press. Here they heard only a run of links, with no count and no boundary. They could not tell how long a post's contents were, or skip past them quickly.

The contents links are now an ordered list, with each link in its own list item. The list's bullets, numbers, margins and padding are reset, so the block looks as it did. Each link fills its row, and on small screens it keeps a 44 px tap target.

On production, all 6 contents links on the accessibility post now sit in a list, and all 9 on the WebMCP post do. A screen reader can announce the contents' length and jump past it.
