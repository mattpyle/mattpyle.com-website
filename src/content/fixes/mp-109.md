The WebMCP post explains how this site gives AI agents tools they can call. It links three times to /webmcp, the page where those tools run. All three links left off the trailing slash.

The page's real address ends in a slash. So each click got a 308 redirect first, then the page. A reader paid an extra round trip to the server every time. Search crawlers saw internal links that point at an address that redirects. And the post broke a rule the site states about itself: every internal link carries the slash.

The fix added the slash to the three links in the post's markdown. The post now holds no link to the redirecting address, and a click goes straight to the tools.
