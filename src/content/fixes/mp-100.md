A visitor who submitted "not a site" to /audit got an error callout above the form. The input still held their text. Nothing in its markup said the address had failed.

A screen reader on the field read "Website address, edit text, not a site" and nothing more. The error belonged to the page, not to the field where the visitor corrects it.

Correcting a typo is the most common thing anyone does on a form. This visitor had to find the callout to learn why the field was refused.

When an address is refused, the input is now marked invalid and points to the error text. The callout's body paragraph carries the id the field refers to.

The refused-address page now serves the invalid marker on the input.
